\begin{abstract}
In thermal-gradient columns, laminar helium carries trace vapours along
a tube whose wall temperature falls steeply. We present an OpenFOAM finite-volume model for nine
Pb--Bi--I gases in a frozen helium carrier, with element-conserving gas
equilibrium and shared wall inventories. Reversible iodide exchange
follows a Hertz--Knudsen--Schrage law in series with the near-wall
diffusive resistance. Published measurements supply temperature
profiles and deposition scans; unreported source histories are
assumptions. Fifteen baseline and 64 follow-up simulations separate
numerical refinement from input sensitivities. The largest raw
element-balance residue is $1.41\times10^{-12}$ of supplied material.
Halving the time step changes iodine profiles by at most 0.323\%, and
eight instead of three correctors by at most 0.021\%. First-order upwind
advection differs from a bounded limited-linear scheme by up to 20.8\%
at equal mesh; with the bounded scheme, the most sensitive profile
changes by 1.98\% from 41,472 to 93,312 cells, without a consistent
observed order. Radial diffusion, not interfacial kinetics, limits wall
exchange. A $+29\units{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$ shift in the BiI gas Gibbs energy
changes the BiI$_3$ share of deposited iodide iodine from 94.58\% to
2.01\%. For silica, shifts derived from inconsistent published
temperature labels reduce 4--5 cm position errors to 0--1 cm without
establishing corrected coordinates. Without tuning, the wall law
reproduces deposition-onset temperatures of five independent PbI$_2$
experiments with an RMS deviation of 18 K, against 35 K for two
published models, although computed peaks lie 1--4 cm upstream. The
results support a conservative method and an explicit assessment of
uncertain inputs, not independent predictive validation.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Data Availability}
The solver, the benchmark preparation scripts and the data that support
the findings of this study are openly available in the LESTO repository
at \url{https://github.com/JanKren/LESTO},
\confirm{archived release or DOI, and that the branch holding these
files is public}. These files comprise the completed baseline,
follow-up, production, scheme and mesh, and iteration-check metrics,
saved axial profiles, generated tables,
figure-generation code and SHA-256 provenance records. The experimental
curves were digitised from Liu et al.\ \cite{Liu2025chemical}; they
reconstruct published figures, are not author-supplied measurements,
and are adapted under the source paper's CC BY 4.0 licence. The
measured profiles, wall temperatures and model curves of Lobresco et
al.\ \cite{Lobresco2026assessment} were digitised in the same way and
are adapted under that paper's CC BY 4.0 licence; the case set-up and
final profiles of our comparison runs are included. Some
thermodynamic coefficient files are external inputs that are not
redistributed; their local identifiers and the provenance of the derived
tables are recorded with the benchmark. Reproducing the CFD calculations
from the coefficients requires access to those inputs, whereas
regenerating the figures from the saved results does not.
