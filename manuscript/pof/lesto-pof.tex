\begin{abstract}
In thermal-gradient columns, laminar helium carries trace vapours
along a tube whose wall temperature falls steeply. Interpreting the
iodine deposits requires a consistent treatment of transport, gas
speciation and wall exchange. We present an OpenFOAM finite-volume
model for nine Pb--Bi--I gases in a frozen helium carrier, with
element-conserving gas equilibrium and shared wall inventories.
Reversible iodide exchange follows a Hertz--Knudsen--Schrage law in
series with the near-wall diffusive resistance. Published
thermosublimatography measurements supply three temperature profiles
and deposition scans; unreported source histories and flow-reference
conditions are treated as assumptions. Fifteen baseline and 64
follow-up simulations separate numerical refinement from selected
input sensitivities. In 60 s follow-up runs, the largest raw
element-balance residue is $8.85\times10^{-13}$ of supplied material.
Halving the time step from 2 to 1 ms changes iodine profiles by at
most 0.323\%, whereas refining the mesh from 18,432 to 41,472 cells
changes them by 2.06--10.58\%; spatial convergence remains unresolved.
A $+29\units{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$ shift in the BiI gas Gibbs energy changes
the BiI$_3$ share of deposited iodide iodine in the refined steel case
from 94.57\% to 2.00\%, a strong but conditional thermochemical
sensitivity. For silica, two shifts derived from discrepancies between
the published temperature curve and its peak labels reduce 4--5 cm
position errors to 0--1 cm, but do not establish a corrected
experimental coordinate system. The results support a conservative
computational method and an explicit assessment of uncertain inputs;
they neither establish independent predictive validation nor reproduce
the three-hour experiment and subsequent cooling.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Data Availability}
The solver, the benchmark preparation scripts and the data that support
the findings of this study are openly available in the LESTO repository
at \url{https://github.com/JanKren/LESTO},
\confirm{archived release or DOI, and that the branch holding these
files is public}. These files comprise the completed baseline and
follow-up metrics, saved axial profiles, generated tables,
figure-generation code and SHA-256 provenance records. The experimental
curves were digitised from Liu et al.\ \cite{Liu2025chemical}; they
reconstruct published figures, are not author-supplied measurements,
and are adapted under the source paper's CC BY 4.0 licence. Some
thermodynamic coefficient files are external inputs that are not
redistributed; their local identifiers and the provenance of the derived
tables are recorded with the benchmark. Reproducing the CFD calculations
from the coefficients requires access to those inputs, whereas
regenerating the figures from the saved results does not.
