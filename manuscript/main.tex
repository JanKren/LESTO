\begin{abstract}
Interpreting iodine deposition in thermal-gradient columns requires a
consistent treatment of transport, gas speciation and exchange with
condensed wall deposits. We present an OpenFOAM finite-volume model
for nine Pb--Bi--I gases in a frozen helium carrier, with
element-conserving gas equilibrium and shared wall inventories.
Reversible iodide exchange follows a Hertz--Knudsen--Schrage law in
series with the diffusive resistance between a cell centre and its
wall face. Published thermosublimatography measurements supply three
temperature profiles and deposition scans; unreported source histories
and flow-reference conditions are treated as assumptions. Fifteen
baseline and 64 follow-up simulations separate numerical refinement
from selected input sensitivities. The baselines use bounded
limited-linear advection on 41,472 cells; the follow-up sensitivities
use first-order upwind advection on 18,432 cells and are reported as
differences from their own baselines. In 60 s runs, the largest raw
element-balance residue is $1.41\times10^{-12}$ of supplied material.
Halving the time step changes iodine deposition profiles by at most
0.323\%, and eight instead of three species correctors by at most
0.021\%. In the most mesh-sensitive case, upwind advection differs from
the bounded scheme by 7.7--20.8\% at equal mesh, and the bounded-scheme
profile changes by 1.98\% from 41,472 to 93,312 cells; apparent orders
are inconsistent, so no formal grid-convergence index is claimed. A
$+29\units{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$ shift in the BiI gas Gibbs energy changes the
BiI$_3$ share of deposited iodide iodine in the steel case from 94.58\%
to 2.01\%, a strong but conditional thermochemical sensitivity. For
silica, two shifts derived from discrepancies between the published
temperature curve and its peak labels reduce 4--5 cm position errors to
0--1 cm, but they do not establish a corrected experimental coordinate
system. Without tuning, the same wall law reproduces the
deposition-onset temperatures of five independent single-species PbI$_2$
experiments with an RMS deviation of 18 K, against 35 K for both
models of the study that reported them, although the computed deposit
peaks lie 1--4 cm upstream. The results support a conservative
computational method and an explicit assessment of uncertain inputs;
they neither establish independent predictive validation nor reproduce
the three-hour experiment and subsequent cooling.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Data and software availability}
The solver and benchmark preparation scripts are maintained in the
LESTO repository, \url{https://github.com/JanKren/LESTO}. The accompanying
working-manuscript files contain the completed baseline, follow-up,
production, scheme and mesh, and iteration-check metrics, saved axial
profiles, generated tables, figure-generation code
and SHA-256 provenance records. Experimental curves were digitised from
Liu et al.\ \cite{Liu2025chemical}; they reconstruct published figures
and are not author-supplied measurements. Figure data are adapted under
the source paper's CC BY 4.0 licence. The measured profiles, wall
temperatures and model curves of Lobresco et al.\
\cite{Lobresco2026assessment} were digitised in the same way and are
adapted under that paper's CC BY 4.0 licence; the case set-up and final
profiles of our comparison runs are included. Some thermodynamic coefficient
files are external inputs and are not redistributed; their local
identifiers and derived-table provenance are recorded with the
benchmark. Reproducing the CFD calculation from coefficients requires
access to those inputs. Regenerating the manuscript figures uses only
the saved results and does not require them.
