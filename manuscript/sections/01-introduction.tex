\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Iodine released from lead--bismuth eutectic (LBE) can travel in several
chemical forms before it deposits on cooler surfaces. The chemical form
matters because it sets both the gas-phase inventory available for
transport and the equilibrium pressure that governs wall exchange.
Liu et al.\ \cite{Liu2025chemical} studied this behaviour by evaporating
pure iodides and iodine-containing LBE into helium, separating the
products along a negative temperature gradient and characterising the
deposits. Their measurements give spatially resolved evidence for
PbI$_2$ and BiI$_3$ deposition, and for potassium-containing deposits in
some LBE experiments. Linking such measurements to a transport
calculation needs more than a single deposition temperature: the
temperature-coordinate mapping, the carrier flow, the source history
and the gas composition all affect the predicted distribution.

Seen as a fluid-dynamics problem, a thermal-gradient column is a slow
laminar flow of an inert carrier through a long, narrow tube whose wall
temperature falls by several hundred kelvin along its length
(\cref{fig:temperature}). Trace vapours released near the hot end are
advected downstream by a carrier whose density and velocity vary with
the imposed temperature, and they diffuse radially towards the wall.
Where the local vapour pressure exceeds the equilibrium pressure at the
wall temperature, material is lost to the wall; where it falls below
that pressure, existing deposits can evaporate again. The deposition
profile is therefore set by the competition between axial advection,
radial diffusion to the wall and the interfacial exchange law. This is
a Graetz-type wall mass-transfer problem \cite{Shah1978circular}, but
with a nonlinear wall condition that depends on the local wall
temperature and condensed inventory. The same coupling underlies the
interpretation of thermochromatographic experiments
\cite{Eichler1975verfluchtigung,Zvara1985simulation,Zvara2008inorganic}.

Existing CFD treatments of such wall exchange differ mainly in where the
exchange is applied and in how many coupled species they carry.
Lobresco et al.\ \cite{Lobresco2026assessment} compared a calibrated
temperature-based deposition law with a vapour-pressure-driven
Hertz--Knudsen--Schrage law for PbI$_2$, both formulated as volumetric
source terms rather than boundary fluxes. For steam condensation with
non-condensable gases, Vijaya Kumar et al.\ \cite{VijayaKumar2021implementation}
implemented a diffusion-layer wall model in the OpenFOAM-based
containmentFOAM both as volumetric sources and as face fluxes, and found
the face-flux form more accurate, cheaper and less sensitive to the
grid. Mixed (Robin) deposition boundary conditions for fission-product
transport in OpenFOAM have been verified against analytical solutions
\cite{DiRonco2021eulerian}. In these studies each depositing or
condensing species is transported on its own; gas-phase changes of
chemical form are not coupled to the wall exchange.

This paper adds that coupling, in the finite-volume framework of
OpenFOAM \cite{Weller1998tensorial}. Our contribution has three parts.
First, element-conserving homogeneous equilibrium among nine Pb--Bi--I
gases is coupled to reversible face-flux wall exchange, in which the
interfacial kinetic conductance acts in series with diffusion over the
distance from the cell centre to the wall; the Graetz--Robin
verification shows that this face flux is second-order accurate where
the same kinetic law applied to the cell-centre value, the form of a
first-cell sink, is first order. Second, gas pathways that
lead to the same condensed material share one physical wall inventory,
so that switching between deposition, evaporation and reaction cannot
create or duplicate material. Third, we characterise the transport
behaviour that results in these columns: wall exchange limited by
radial diffusion rather than by interfacial kinetics, which a sweep of
the wall Damk\"ohler number over six decades confirms by reproducing
both limits of the exchange and the transition between them; profiles
whose local shape is far more resolution-sensitive than their
integrals; and an iodide partition that is controlled by thermodynamic
input.
The software also has an interface to the GEMS3K kernel of
GEM-Selektor \cite{Kulik2013gemselektor}, but the reported calculations
use a specialised ideal-gas element-potential solver. Repeating one
production case with GEMS3K standard-state data in every cell changes
its deposit profiles by at most $2.1\times10^{-4}$ (relative $L_1$); a
mode that also calls the GEMS3K solver in every cell
($5.1\times10^{8}$ calls, none failed) gives bitwise identical results,
because that solution only initialises the element-potential solve.

All experimental input comes from published papers, so we report the
consequences of two explicit source models and of selected input
variations rather than infer a unique physical source. The study addresses three questions. Can
transport, gas equilibrium and competing wall channels be coupled while
preserving each represented element? How strongly do deposition
profiles depend on time step, iteration, mesh and advection scheme,
including when total deposition appears stable? Which input changes
materially alter the interpretation of the computed deposits? The
evidence comprises 79 computational cases of the three available Liu et
al.\ configurations (not experimental replicates), production re-runs
with a bounded advection scheme, a scheme and mesh study, an iteration
check, the Graetz--Robin verification, and an untuned comparison with
five single-species experiments of Lobresco et al.\ and with the two
models of that study. The scope is the executed model: a fixed carrier
and temperature field, prescribed release, ideal-gas speciation, and
wall deposits without aerosol formation or substrate chemistry
(\cref{sec:limitations}). Comparisons with experiment are conditional
assessments of this model, not independent predictive validation.

\Cref{sec:experiments,sec:model,sec:numerics} define the reconstructed
inputs, equations and case matrix. \Cref{sec:verification} verifies the
wall discretisation and assesses conservation and resolution, and
\cref{sec:comparison,sec:sensitivity} examine experimental
discrepancies and input sensitivity. \Cref{sec:discussion} states what
the evidence supports and collects the limitations.
