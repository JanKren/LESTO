\section{Transport, speciation and wall exchange}
\label{sec:model}
\subsection{Frozen carrier and trace-species equations}
The carrier fields $\rho$, $\vect{u}$, $T$ and $p$ are prepared from a
steady laminar helium calculation and then held fixed. The reconstructed
axial temperature is imposed throughout each cross-section. A gas species
$i$ is represented by $Y_i$, its mass per unit carrier mass, and satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\partial(\rho Y_i)}{\partial t}
 +\nabla\cdot(\rho\vect{u}Y_i)
 =\nabla\cdot(\rho D_i\nabla Y_i)+S_i^{\rm rel}+S_i^{\rm wall}.
 \label{eq:transport}
\end{equation}
Gas equilibrium is applied as a local redistribution between transport
steps. Species do not alter the carrier density, momentum or temperature.
The represented gases are Pb, PbI, PbI$_2$, Bi, Bi$_2$, BiI, BiI$_3$,
I and I$_2$. Helium is inert. Condensed PbI$_2$, BiI$_3$, Pb and Bi
are stored as immobile wall inventories. The solid-state identifiers in
the code label these reservoirs; the equilibrium-pressure tables select
between crystalline and liquid states where applicable.

No bulk nucleation, aerosols, thermal diffusion, hydrogen/oxygen chemistry,
latent-heat feedback or chemical reaction with the steel or silica
substrate is represented. Consequently, differences between the column
cases arise from their prescribed thermal profiles, dimensions and source
inputs, rather than a fitted substrate-specific sticking law.

\subsection{Homogeneous ideal-gas equilibrium}
\label{sec:equilibrium}
Let $\nu_{ki}$ be the number of atoms of element $k\in\{\mathrm{Pb},
\mathrm{Bi},\mathrm{I}\}$ in gas $i$. At fixed local temperature the
transported element concentrations are
\begin{equation}
 b_k=\sum_i\nu_{ki}\frac{\rho Y_i}{M_i}.
 \label{eq:elements}
\end{equation}
The equilibrium solver seeks gas partial pressures and dimensionless
element potentials $\lambda_k$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
 \frac{p_i}{p^\circ}=K_{f,i}(T)
 \exp\!\left(\sum_k\nu_{ki}\lambda_k\right),
 \qquad
 \sum_i\nu_{ki}\frac{p_i}{RT}=b_k,
 \qquad p^\circ=10^5\units{Pa}.
 \label{eq:equilibrium}
\end{equation}
The tabulated $K_{f,i}$ use a common elemental reference and the stated
pressure standard. Concentrations convert back through
$Y_i=M_i p_i/(\rho RT)$. This enforces elemental rather than individual
species conservation during re-speciation. The helium pressure is not
an additional reactive element constraint; its field remains prescribed
under the trace-species approximation.

The executed solver uses logarithmic variables and a safeguarded Newton
iteration. Pb and Bi subproblems are reduced at fixed iodine potential,
with a Schur-complement treatment of the remaining coupling. Canonical
initialisation avoids dependence on an unsaved equilibrium warm state
when restarting a case. Re-speciation is requested every time step for
cells with $T\ge350\units{K}$; below this threshold the transported gas
composition is retained. This cutoff is a numerical modelling choice,
not a demonstration that reactions cease at 350 K. Homogeneous condensed
phases are excluded, and instantaneous gas equilibrium substitutes for
unmeasured finite reaction rates.

\subsection{Reversible iodide exchange}
\label{sec:wall}
For PbI$_2$ and BiI$_3$, the kinetic wall law uses a positive flux from
condensate into gas:
\begin{equation}
 j_i=G_i\,[p_{{\rm eq},i}(T_w)-p_{i,w}],
 \qquad
 G_i=C_e s_i\frac{2\alpha_i}{2-\alpha_i}
       \sqrt{\frac{M_i}{2\pi RT_w}}.
 \label{eq:hks}
\end{equation}
Here $j_i$ is in $\mathrm{kg\,m^{-2}\,s^{-1}}$, $M_i$ is in
$\mathrm{kg\,mol^{-1}}$, pressures are in Pa, $\alpha_i$ is the
accommodation coefficient and $C_e$, $s_i$ are dimensionless
multipliers. The baseline uses $C_e=s_i=\alpha_i=1$.
This is the Hertz--Knudsen--Schrage form used in vapour-pressure-driven
deposition modelling \cite{Lobresco2026assessment}. The condensed-state
pressure is calculated from the gas and condensed Gibbs energies, using
the stable tabulated condensed branch. The wall temperature, rather than
a prescribed deposition threshold, selects that pressure.

The kinetic law must also communicate with the cell-centre concentration
through molecular diffusion. With $d_f$ the cell-centre-to-wall distance,
$p_{i,c}=\chi_{i,c}Y_{i,c}$ and
$\chi_{i,c}=\rho_cRT_c/M_i$, elimination of the wall concentration gives
\begin{equation}
 G_{i,f}^{\rm eff}=
 \frac{G_i}{1+G_i\chi_{i,c}d_f/(\rho D_i)_f},
 \qquad
 j_{i,f}=G_{i,f}^{\rm eff}
 [p_{{\rm eq},i}(T_w)-\chi_{i,c}Y_{i,c}].
 \label{eq:halfcell}
\end{equation}
This combines kinetic and half-cell diffusion resistances in series.
The gas equation receives the face flow $A_f j_{i,f}$ as an implicit
cell contribution, while the wall reservoir receives its negative.
Evaporation is limited by the available face inventory over the time
step; a bare wall cannot evaporate. Inventory updates use the exchange
actually realised by the final gas solve, including active bounds,
rather than an independently evaluated explicit predictor. Reversal
between deposition and evaporation therefore does not create material.

\subsection{Shared metal inventories and a reactive channel}
Additional wall channels connect Pb(g) to condensed Pb and Bi(g) and
Bi$_2$(g) to a common condensed Bi inventory, with their own equilibrium
pressures and stoichiometric mass conversion. They use the baseline
interfacial coefficients. The reactive channel
\begin{equation}
 3\ce{BiI}(g)\longrightarrow 2\ce{Bi}(\mathrm{condensed})
                          +\ce{BiI3}(g)
 \label{eq:disproportionation}
\end{equation}
is enabled only in the forward direction in the executed cases. For
unit activity of condensed Bi, the dimensionless reaction constant and
effective reactant equilibrium pressure are
\begin{align}
 K_r(T_w)&=\exp\left[-\frac{2G_{\rm Bi,c}^\circ+
 G_{\rm BiI_3,g}^\circ-3G_{\rm BiI,g}^\circ}{RT_w}\right],\\
 p_{{\rm eq},\rm BiI}&=p^\circ
 \left[\frac{p_{\rm BiI_3,c}/p^\circ}{K_r(T_w)}\right]^{1/3},\\
 j_{\rm BiI}&=\min\{0,\,G_{\rm BiI}^{\rm eff}
                  [p_{{\rm eq},\rm BiI}-p_{\rm BiI,c}]\}.
 \label{eq:reactive-flux}
\end{align}
All Gibbs energies in $K_r$ are evaluated at $T_w$.
This is a phenomenological HKS-conductance treatment of the reaction
driving force, with the same half-cell resistance as the other channels.
The coproduct pressure is evaluated from the beginning-of-step gas
field, and the minimum suppresses reverse reaction. The formula consumes three
BiI molecules, supplies one BiI$_3$ molecule to the gas and adds two Bi
atoms to the shared wall reservoir. All channels access that same Bi
inventory; a separate inventory for each reaction would duplicate a
physical material. Coproducts are applied after the transported reactants
have been solved. This irreversible channel is a model assumption and
must not be interpreted as a fully reversible surface-equilibrium model.

\subsection{Prescribed release constructions}
\label{sec:source}
Source model Q1 releases PbI$_2$ and, for LBE, BiI$_3$ at constant rates
\begin{equation}
 q_{1,i}=\frac{m_i}{M_i\tau_{\rm exp}},
 \qquad \tau_{\rm exp}=10{,}800\units{s}.
 \label{eq:q1}
\end{equation}
For pure PbI$_2$, $m_i=14.6\units{mg}$ is the initial sample mass;
complete uniform release over the experimental period is assumed.
For steel LBE, $m_{\rm PbI_2}=0.111\units{mg}$ and
$m_{\rm BiI_3}=0.181\units{mg}$; for silica LBE, they are 0.243 and
0.085 mg. These compound masses were inferred from deposited material
in the experimental paper. Their use conditions both iodine loading and
much of the Q1 species split on the measurements.

Source model Q2 supplies atomic Pb, Bi and I to the gas-equilibrium
system. With $q_{\rm He}$ the full-column helium molar flow and $p_h$
the source pressure, its elemental rates are
\begin{align}
 q_{2,\rm Pb}&=q_{1,\rm PbI_2}
             +q_{\rm He}\beta p_{\rm Pb}(T_h)/p_h,\\
 q_{2,\rm Bi}&=q_{1,\rm BiI_3}
             +q_{\rm He}\beta[p_{\rm Bi}(T_h)+2p_{\rm Bi_2}(T_h)]/p_h,\\
 q_{2,\rm I}&=2q_{1,\rm PbI_2}+3q_{1,\rm BiI_3}.
 \label{eq:q2}
\end{align}
The metal pressures use ideal LBE activities $a_{\rm Pb}=0.447$ and
$a_{\rm Bi}=0.553$ at $T_h=1173.15\units{K}$. The multiplicative
metal saturation $\beta$ is unmeasured. Q2 changes the metal supply and
allows the iodide split to emerge from the model, but still fixes its
iodine source from deposited iodine. It is therefore a source hypothesis,
not independently identified LBE evaporation. Rates are distributed over
an assumed source region without resolving a liquid surface, depletion
of the physical boat or its obstruction of the flow.
