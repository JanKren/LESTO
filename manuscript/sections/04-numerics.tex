\section{Numerical implementation and study design}
\label{sec:numerics}
This section describes the geometry and carrier preparation, the
discretisation and coupling, and the completed case matrix.

\subsection{Geometry and carrier preparation}
A structured axisymmetric 5$^\circ$ wedge represents the 1.25 m column
(\cref{fig:geometry}). The steel and silica radii are 2.4 and 2.5 mm.
The production mesh has $576\times72\times1=41{,}472$ cells. The
64 follow-up sensitivities use the original
$384\times48\times1=18{,}432$-cell mesh. The mesh studies use
$256\times32\times1=8{,}192$, 18,432, 41,472 and, for one case,
$864\times108\times1=93{,}312$ cells; successive levels refine the
axial and radial spacing by a factor of 1.5. The source occupies
$x=0.09$--0.105 m and the radial interval $r=0.25R$--$0.75R$. Its
position and cell selection are computational assumptions, and the
physical boat is omitted from the carrier geometry. Full-column source
and inlet rates are multiplied by $5/360=1/72$ for the wedge.

The inlet flow rates are converted to helium mass flow assuming a
controller reference of 298.15 K and 101,325 Pa. The supplied paper
does not specify these reference conditions. Full-column rates are
$2.727\times10^{-7}\units{kg\,s^{-1}}$ at 100 mL/min and
$1.227\times10^{-7}\units{kg\,s^{-1}}$ at 45 mL/min. A separate study
uses 273.15 K at the same reference pressure. Helium follows the
perfect-gas law with constant heat capacity and Sutherland viscosity
(\cref{app:properties}). Steady laminar carriers are computed with
\texttt{rhoSimpleFoam}, while the reconstructed temperature profile
stays prescribed.

The converged face mass flux is kept, rather than reconstructed from
interpolated velocity. At these low mass flows, a zero-initialised
potential correction removes residual discrete divergence before
species transport. Carrier preparation rejects a carrier if its
original cell or global flux defect exceeds $10^{-6}$ of the inlet
flow, or if a flux or velocity correction exceeds $10^{-5}$ of the
corresponding scale. After correction, inlet, global and cell continuity and leakage
through impermeable boundaries are checked against $10^{-12}$ of the
inlet flow. This procedure makes the frozen transport field discretely
conservative. It is neither a new physical flow model nor evidence that
the velocity field has converged.

The stated inputs fix the transport regime. With the full-column
helium mass flow $\dot m$, the tube diameter $d$ and the Sutherland
viscosity of \cref{app:properties}, $\mathrm{Re}=4\dot m/(\pi d\mu)$ is
1.5--3.0 for the 100 mL/min steel case and 0.7--1.3 for the 45 mL/min
cases between the source-furnace temperature of 1173 K and 400 K; the
carrier is laminar throughout. With the baseline diffusivity, the
radial diffusion time $R^2/D_i$ is 58 ms in steel and 63 ms in silica.
Placing the conductance of \cref{eq:hks} in series with diffusion over
the tube radius, as in \cref{eq:halfcell} with $d_f$ replaced by $R$
and $T_c=T_w$, defines a wall Damk\"ohler number
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathrm{Da}_i&=\frac{G_i\chi_iR}{\rho D_i}\\
 &=C_es_i\frac{2\alpha_i}{2-\alpha_i}
  \sqrt{\frac{RT_w}{2\pi M_i}}\,\frac{R}{D_i},
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:damkohler}
\end{equation}
where $R$ is the gas constant under the square root and the tube
radius in the last factor. Because $\chi_i/\rho=RT_w/M_i$, it does not
depend on pressure. For
PbI$_2$ and BiI$_3$ between 400 and 1173 K, the baseline coefficients
give $\mathrm{Da}_i\approx1.4\times10^3$--$2.9\times10^3$, and the
tested accommodation coefficients $\alpha_i=0.1$ and 0.01 give about
76--150 and 7--15. This tube-scale estimate places the interfacial
resistance well below the radial diffusion resistance for
$\alpha_i=1$ and 0.1, and about an order of magnitude below it for
$\alpha_i=0.01$. It is a scaling estimate, not a substitute for the
resolved near-wall gradients of the calculations.

Two effects that the model omits can be bounded with the same inputs,
the digitised wall profiles and the computed absolute pressure of
101.33 kPa. The first is buoyancy. Neither experimental paper states
the column orientation. For developed laminar flow with a linearly
varying wall temperature, the wall-to-bulk temperature difference is
$(11/192)\,U d^2|\mathrm{d}T_w/\mathrm{d}x|/a$, with $a$ the thermal
diffusivity of helium; it stays below 2.6 K. A Grashof number based
on the axial temperature change over one diameter,
$\mathrm{Gr}=g d^4|\mathrm{d}T_w/\mathrm{d}x|/(T\nu^2)$, stays below
2.2, and below 1.4 where the wall temperature lies between 400 and
750 K. Because Re is also of order one, the Richardson number
$\mathrm{Gr}/\mathrm{Re}^2$ reaches 0.15 (pure PbI$_2$) to 0.78
(silica) in that range, and about 1.2 in the steepest cold section of
the silica profile. Compositional buoyancy at the largest computed
trace mole fraction, $9.2\times10^{-5}$ evaluated with the molar mass
of BiI$_3$, gives $\mathrm{Gr}/\mathrm{Re}^2\le0.32$. Buoyancy-driven
secondary flow is therefore weak, but in a horizontal column it cannot
be excluded, and the frozen carrier does not represent it. The second
effect is thermal (Soret) diffusion. Taking a thermal diffusion factor
$\alpha_T=1$ as a generous bound, the Soret drift velocity
$D_i\alpha_T|\mathrm{d}T_w/\mathrm{d}x|/T$ is at most 2.2\% of the
carrier velocity. The radial Soret flux, relative to a Fickian flux
$x_iD_i/R$ towards the wall, is at most $\alpha_T\Delta T_r/T\le0.7\%$,
with $\Delta T_r$ the wall-to-bulk difference above. Both are small
compared with the factor-of-two diffusivity variations examined in
\cref{sec:sensitivity}.

\subsection{Discretisation and coupling}
The transient species equations use implicit Euler time integration
and corrected Gauss linear diffusion. The production cases (the 15
baselines and four silica temperature-coordinate cases) use the bounded
OpenFOAM scheme \texttt{Gauss limitedLinear 1}
\cite{Jasak1996error,Sweby1984high} for advection on 41,472 cells. It
blends linear and upwind interpolation through a TVD-type flux limiter,
which reverts to upwinding near extrema and steep gradients. The 64 follow-up cases and the time-step
comparisons keep the first-order upwind advection of the original
campaign on 18,432 cells, and the follow-up sensitivities are always
reported as differences from their own upwind baselines on that mesh.
The scheme and mesh study adds the van Leer limiter
\cite{vanLeer1974towards} (\texttt{Gauss vanLeer}) as a second bounded
scheme. All nine transported gases use the same advection scheme
within a case. The baseline time
step is 2 ms. Wall exchange is assembled implicitly with nonnegative
source and sink coefficients and shared inventory bounds. Configured
species correctors and active-bound guards reconcile the final gas
solution with the realised wall transfer. Gas re-speciation then
redistributes species under the local element constraints. The
sequence is first order in time; a conservative equilibrium projection
alone does not remove splitting or transport error.

The baseline configuration requests three species correctors with a
$10^{-16}$ tolerance and up to eight bound guards with a $10^{-13}$
guard tolerance. These are stopping settings. They do not guarantee
that every outer wall-regime iteration reaches the requested
tolerance, so the final transport defect, bound status and material
ledger are examined separately (\cref{sec:conservation}). Short
corrector replays and three full runs with eight correctors test the
effect of this stopping criterion (\cref{sec:conservation}).

Every gas has the baseline diffusivity $D_i=10^{-4}\units{m^2\,s^{-1}}$.
Three transport alternatives halve or double this value for all gases,
or replace only the PbI$_2$ coefficient with a temperature- and
pressure-dependent PbI$_2$--He estimate. The collision parameters of
that estimate are themselves estimates, not independently measured
binary transport data. Accommodation variations set $\alpha=0.1$ or
0.01 for the two reversible iodide pairs and keep the baseline
metal-channel coefficients.

\subsection{Completed case matrix}
The 15 baseline cases are Q1 for each available configuration, plus Q2
for the two LBE columns at $\beta\in\{0.001,0.01,0.1\}$ with two
thermodynamic branches. The second branch adds a constant 29 kJ/mol to
the standard Gibbs energy of BiI(g). It is a sensitivity surrogate, not
a second experimentally qualified dataset. All completed calculations
use the same solver executable, identified by its SHA-256 in the saved
reports.

\Cref{tab:study} lists the 64 follow-up cases. The studies cover the
following cases:
\begin{itemize}
\item mesh and time-step checks: pure PbI$_2$ and three steel Q2 cases
  (baseline thermodynamics at 0.1\% and 1\% metal saturation, and
  shifted BiI thermodynamics at 1\%);
\item physical transport, accommodation, source-position and
  flow-reference variations: pure PbI$_2$, the two steel Q2 cases with
  baseline thermodynamics, and silica Q2 at 0.1\%;
\item extra source-loading cases: both columns and both thermodynamic
  branches;
\item temperature-coordinate variants: silica Q1 and silica Q2 at
  0.1\%.
\end{itemize}
This is a structured set of conditional comparisons, not a factorial
uncertainty study.

The source-position alternatives move the release region by
$\pm1.5\units{cm}$ without adding a boat obstruction. The flow-stop
cases restart from the saved 60 s fields, remove injection, set the
carrier flow to zero and impose zero normal species flux at both ends.
The original nonuniform temperature profile stays fixed. These cases
therefore measure how residual gas redistributes during a 20 s flow
stop at fixed temperature; they are neither a clean-helium purge nor a
full cooling calculation.

Four further sets of runs complete the evidence, all with the same
solver executable. (i) The \ProductionCases{} production cases repeat
the 15 baselines and the four silica temperature-coordinate cases on
41,472 cells with the limited-linear scheme; all other inputs are
unchanged. (ii) A scheme and mesh study takes two cases, pure PbI$_2$
and the shifted-BiI steel case at 1\% metal saturation, which had the
largest upwind fine/medium change. It runs them with upwind,
limited-linear and van Leer advection on 8,192 to 41,472 cells, and the
steel case with upwind and limited-linear also on 93,312 cells. This
adds 12 runs to the existing upwind levels and the production runs.
(iii) Three original 18,432-cell upwind cases are repeated over the full
60 s with eight instead of three species correctors. (iv) Ten runs
simulate five single-species PbI$_2$ experiments of Lobresco et al.\
\cite{Lobresco2026assessment} on two meshes (\cref{sec:lobresco}).
