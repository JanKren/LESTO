\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}
We have applied a conservative finite-volume treatment of nine
Pb--Bi--I gases and four shared condensed wall inventories, with
element-conserving gas equilibrium and reversible face-flux wall
exchange, to 15 baseline and 64 follow-up cases, 19 production re-runs
and a scheme, mesh and iteration study. The columns are laminar helium
flows ($\mathrm{Re}\approx0.7$--3) in tubes with a steep axial
wall-temperature gradient, where axial advection, radial diffusion and
the interfacial exchange law together set the deposition profile. The
worst raw element residue is $8.85\times10^{-13}$ of supplied material
in the follow-up cases and $\ProductionResidue$ in the production
cases; conservation is a numerical property of the model, not evidence
that all physical pathways are represented. On a Graetz--Robin problem
the half-cell wall law converges at second order to the reference decay
rate, whereas applying the kinetic law to the cell-centre value is first
order; this verifies the wall discretisation, not the convergence of
the iodine calculations. Within the model, radial diffusion rather than
interfacial kinetics limits the wall exchange: the wall Damk\"ohler
number is large, and the profiles respond far more to the diffusivity
than to the accommodation coefficient.

Integral results are much less sensitive to resolution than local
profiles. Halving the time step changes iodine profiles by at most
\TimeMax\%, and eight instead of three species correctors by at most
\IterSteelHigh\%. The spatial error is larger and is dominated by the
numerical diffusion of first-order upwind advection, which differs
from the limited-linear scheme by \UpLLFour--\UpLLOne\% at equal mesh in
the most sensitive case, whereas two bounded schemes differ by at most
\VLSteelMax\%. With limited-linear advection that case changes by
\SteelLLThree\% between 41,472 and 93,312 cells, but the apparent orders
are inconsistent, so we quote this change rather than a formal grid
convergence index. Closely competing 1 cm bins can shift a reported
peak temperature by tens of kelvin, so peaks are reported with their
smoothed maximum and centroid temperature as well.

The computed iodide partition depends strongly on thermodynamic input. In the
refined steel case, a $+29$ kJ/mol shift of the BiI gas Gibbs energy
changes the BiI$_3$ iodine share from 94.58\% to 2.01\%, and a
comparable separation persists at every tested resolution. This shows how much the prediction depends on the BiI data;
it is neither an uncertainty interval nor a criterion for choosing a
dataset. For silica, temperature-coordinate shifts derived from the
published curve and labels reduce 4--5 cm position discrepancies to
0--1 cm. This demonstrates an inconsistency in the published coordinates
without resolving it; the overlap ranking of the two shifts even
reverses between advection schemes.

On five single-species PbI$_2$ experiments of Lobresco et al., the
untuned model gives deposition-onset temperatures with an RMS deviation
of \LobRMS{} K, against \LobRMSTemp{} and \LobRMSVap{} K for the two
models of that study. The result is promising but conditional: it
depends on applying the onset rule to the main deposit (from the inlet,
the RMS would be \LobRMSInlet{} K), the near-source mole fraction of
0.33--0.36 violates the dilute frozen-carrier assumption, and the
release history is unconstrained below 6 s. Because carrier,
vapour-pressure and source assumptions also differ from the published
models, the improvement cannot yet be attributed to the wall treatment,
and the computed peaks lie \LobShiftMin--\LobShiftMax{} cm upstream
with summed profile differences of \LobLOneMin--\LobLOneMax{} of the
sample amount.

The evidence supports a conservative method with a verified wall
discretisation and an explicit assessment of uncertain inputs, not
independent predictive validation. The requirements for the latter are
listed in \cref{sec:limitations}.
