\section{Input sensitivity and interpretation}
\label{sec:sensitivity}
This section examines how source loading, BiI thermodynamic data, the
silica temperature coordinate, and transport, kinetic and flow inputs
change the computed deposits.

\subsection{Source loading and thermodynamic information}
The Q2 cases expose a sensitivity that is hidden when the iodide split
is prescribed. \Cref{fig:thermo} shows the BiI$_3$ share of deposited
iodide iodine,
\begin{equation}
 f_{\rm BiI_3}=
 \frac{3N^{\rm wall}_{\rm BiI_3}}
 {2N^{\rm wall}_{\rm PbI_2}+3N^{\rm wall}_{\rm BiI_3}}.
 \label{eq:split}
\end{equation}
This share counts only the iodine in the two modelled deposited
iodides. It differs from the BiI$_3$ fraction of all experimental
iodine, which also contains KI. Additional metal-saturation cases fill
selected points between the original 0.1\% and 1\% levels; line
segments connect computed points and are not a fitted release law.

At 1\% metal saturation on the production steel mesh, the baseline
thermodynamics give $f_{\rm BiI_3}=94.58\%$ (94.57\% with upwind
advection). Raising the BiI(g) standard Gibbs energy by 29 kJ/mol gives
2.01\% (2.00\%) at the same numerical resolution. This large difference survives refinement. It therefore
reflects conditional thermodynamic sensitivity, not numerical noise.
The perturbation is applied consistently to the formation and
reactive-wall constants. A constant Gibbs-energy shift scales the
formation constant by $\exp[-29{,}000/(RT)]$, approximately 0.051 at
1173 K and 0.00093 at 500 K. It is a substantial surrogate
perturbation, not an independently measured correction to BiI
thermodynamics.

An independent integration of the saved species profiles gives a
BiI$_3$ iodine share of 94.532--94.569\% for the baseline branch and
1.922--2.004\% for the shifted branch in the original upwind campaign over the three meshes and the
medium-mesh half-step run. Comparing the two branches at each matched
resolution gives separations of 92.555--92.619 percentage points.
The qualitative thermodynamic response therefore persists across every
tested resolution. These ranges describe the executed cases; they are
not confidence intervals or extrapolated continuum bounds.

Source loading also matters. With baseline thermodynamics, the steel
BiI$_3$ share at 0.2\%, 0.316\% and 0.562\% metal saturation
(follow-up cases, 18,432 cells, upwind) is
52.50\%, 70.58\% and 94.56\%; the shifted branch gives 47.66\%,
37.43\% and 15.88\%. The low-loading trend is not universally
monotonic. The original steel 0.1\% case with baseline thermodynamics
gives 56.48\% and an 80.66\% scan overlap (56.47\% and 81.10\% on the
18,432-cell upwind mesh). This makes it a useful
diagnostic comparison, but not an identified physical saturation. The
published deposits alone cannot uniquely separate source loading,
thermodynamic data and omitted chemistry. Choosing a branch because it
best matches one scan would introduce calibration and would require
independent validation.

\subsection{Silica temperature-coordinate discrepancy}
\label{sec:silica}
In the silica experiment, the published peak labels do not match the
published temperature curve. The scan places the PbI$_2$ and BiI$_3$
maxima at 51 and 59 cm, labelled 306$\pm$24 and 127$\pm$39\,$^\circ$C.
At those positions the digitised temperature curve gives approximately
369 and 235\,$^\circ$C, which differ from the labels by 63 and 108 K.
On the same curve, 306 and 127\,$^\circ$C occur at 55.096 and 63.653 cm.
The discrepancy exceeds the nominal raster temperature resolution and
suggests a temperature-coordinate inconsistency in the supplied inputs.
It does not identify which measurement, annotation or registration
needs correction.

Two diagnostic mappings shift only the silica temperature curve
upstream, by 4.096 or 4.653 cm, so that one of these curve crossings
aligns with the corresponding labelled position. Equivalently,
$T_\delta(x)=T_0(x+\delta)$ for $\delta>0$, with
$\Delta x_T=-\delta$. The iodine scan stays fixed. The shifts are
derived from the published curve and labels, not optimised against the
CFD profiles. All original unshifted results are retained.

\Cref{tab:alignment,fig:alignment} show that the shifts move the Q1
maxima from 56/63 cm to 51/59 or 51/58 cm and raise the normalised
overlap from 6.13\% to 77.80\% or 73.26\%. The low-loading Q2 variants
place the maxima at 51/59 or 51/58 cm, with overlaps of 77.88\% and
75.54\%. On the 18,432-cell upwind mesh the order of the two shifts was
the opposite (75.29\% and 81.00\% for Q1; 79.16\% and 83.33\% for Q2),
so the ranking of the two hypotheses by overlap is not robust to the
advection scheme and should not be used to prefer one of them.
A plausible coordinate change thus has a larger effect than the tested
source-position or time-step changes. This does not confirm either
shift as the experimental truth. The two shifts also give different
peak temperatures, and no uniquely established mapping reconciles both
original temperature labels at once.

The shifted low-loading Q2 cases keep a BiI$_3$ share near 23.97\%,
about five percentage points below the published iodide-only split.
Correct peak positions therefore do not ensure the correct partition.
Resolving the discrepancy requires the original thermocouple profile
and its coordinate registration, not selection of the CFD variant with
the highest overlap.

\subsection{Transport, kinetics, flow reference and flow stop}
\Cref{tab:sensitivity} reports, for each tested category, the range of
iodine profile changes $E_{L1}$ (\cref{eq:numeric-l1}) from the
designated baseline. These are responses to finite hypothetical
changes, not statistical confidence limits. The source-loading row uses
its 1\% reference case. The temperature-coordinate row measures changes
from the original silica input and can approach 200\% when the profile
supports move apart. These baseline-relative changes must not be
confused with the raw discrepancies against the experimental scan.

Diffusion alternatives change profiles by up to
14.20\%. A 273.15 K flow reference raises the inferred helium mass flow
by 9.15\% relative to 298.15 K and changes profiles by up to 10.93\%.
Iodide accommodation changes give up to 4.21\%, whereas moving the
prescribed source region by $\pm1.5$ cm gives at most 0.19\%. This
small source-position response concerns the selected empty-tube source
model; it cannot rule out an effect of a real boat on the flow or
thermal fields. \Cref{fig:physical} shows the maxima for these
categories. They are differences between upwind cases on the same
18,432-cell mesh, which itself differs from the production scheme by
12--14\% for the two cases of the scheme study
(\cref{sec:verification}); how much of that error cancels in the
differences has not been quantified. Where numerical and input changes
have comparable magnitude, the present results do not rank their
physical importance precisely. The tube-scale Damk\"ohler numbers of
\cref{sec:numerics} and the small accommodation response, at most
0.41\% for $\alpha=0.1$ and 4.21\% for $\alpha=0.01$, against
3.83--14.20\% for halving or doubling $D$, indicate that radial diffusion, not
interfacial kinetics, limits the wall exchange in these columns; the
wall temperature acts mainly through the equilibrium pressure.

The four fixed-temperature flow-stop cases change the deposited iodine
profiles by 1.10--4.63\% over 20 s, with centroid shifts of at most
0.0422 cm. Residual gas can therefore alter wall inventories after
release stops. Because these runs neither cool the column nor flush it
with clean helium (\cref{sec:numerics}), they do not predict the actual
end-of-experiment redistribution after the furnaces are turned off.
Likewise, equilibrium speciation and the irreversible BiI wall channel
remain unqualified kinetic approximations.
