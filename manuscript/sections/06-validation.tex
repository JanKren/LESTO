\section{Conditional comparison with deposition measurements}
\label{sec:comparison}
This section compares the calculations, mainly the original Q1 cases,
with the reconstructed deposition scans. The comparisons are
conditional on the reconstructed inputs and assumptions of
\cref{sec:experiments,sec:model}; they are not independent validation.

\subsection{Comparison measures}
The primary comparison uses reconstructed relative iodine yields per
1 cm bin. Let $e_b$ be the experimental percentage and let
\begin{equation}
 m_b=(100-y_{\rm KI})\frac{a_b}{\sum_{\rm full\ column}a_b}
 \label{eq:yield}
\end{equation}
be the modelled percentage, where the stated excluded KI yield is
$y_{\rm KI}=0$, 6.6 or 7.0. Over the common scanned bins $\mathcal B$,
we report the raw discrepancy
\begin{equation}
 L_{\rm raw}=\sum_{b\in\mathcal B}|m_b-e_b|
 \quad\text{(percentage points)}.
 \label{eq:raw-l1}
\end{equation}
This measure keeps the experimental digitisation sum and the model's
explicit KI exclusion. It is neither an absolute deposited-mass error
nor a percentage relative to an experimental uncertainty.

A secondary shape diagnostic normalises both profiles over
$\mathcal B$, $\widehat m_b=m_b/\sum_{\mathcal B}m_b$ and
$\widehat e_b=e_b/\sum_{\mathcal B}e_b$, and computes the overlap
\begin{equation}
 O=100\sum_{b\in\mathcal B}\min(\widehat m_b,\widehat e_b).
 \label{eq:overlap}
\end{equation}
The overlap $O$ is conditional on the common scanned range and discards
differences in capture and total yield. The experimental normalisation
still contains KI. $O$ is a descriptive shape measure, not a validation
probability or a fitted goodness-of-fit statistic.

\subsection{Q1 peaks and complete profiles}
\Cref{tab:peaks} compares the five available Q1 iodide maxima with the
printed peak temperatures. Besides the 1 cm binned maximum it lists
the maximum of the 3 cm smoothed profile ($x_3$, $T_3$) and the wall
temperature at the deposit centroid ($T_{\rm c}$). All calculated binned
peak temperatures lie within the stated temperature ranges. For pure
PbI$_2$ the binned maximum (36 cm, 366.5\,$^\circ$C) matches the
published 36 cm and 368\,$^\circ$C, but the smoothed maximum and the
centroid temperature (350.5 and 348.7\,$^\circ$C) lie at the
low-temperature edge (350\,$^\circ$C) of the printed range. For steel
PbI$_2$ the binned value is 288.1\,$^\circ$C, whereas the smoothed and
centroid temperatures, 269.2 and 274.5\,$^\circ$C, lie at the lower
limit of the printed 288$\pm$18\,$^\circ$C. The steel BiI$_3$
smoothed maximum (138.8\,$^\circ$C) falls just below its range,
while the centroid temperature (158.1\,$^\circ$C) lies inside it. The
agreement of a binned peak temperature therefore depends on which of
two nearly equal bins is the larger (\cref{sec:verification}). Steel LBE places both iodide
maxima at the measured positions, whereas silica LBE places the
PbI$_2$ and BiI$_3$ maxima 5 and 4 cm downstream, respectively.
Agreement with a printed temperature is therefore not sufficient to
establish the correct spatial location.

\Cref{fig:baseline} shows the complete profiles. The raw discrepancies
$L_{\rm raw}$ are 46.33, 52.97 and 183.84 percentage points for pure
PbI$_2$, steel LBE and silica LBE (53.90, 53.45 and 181.84 on the
18,432-cell upwind mesh), and the corresponding normalised overlaps are
76.97\%, 72.13\% and 6.13\% (72.93\%, 71.88\% and 7.20\%). The silica case therefore
has a large profile discrepancy even though both printed temperature
ranges contain its calculated maxima.

The Q1 steel BiI$_3$ share of deposited iodide iodine is 65.34\%, close
to the measured iodide-only share of $61.3/(61.3+32.1)=65.63\%$.
For silica the corresponding values are 28.68\% and
$27/(27+66)=29.03\%$. These agreements are substantially conditioned
by the source masses, which were inferred from the same deposits. They
cannot serve as independent validation of gas speciation or LBE
release. Similarly, the source-driven calculations capture
91.28--98.20\% of the supplied iodine on the wall by 60 s. This does
not establish the experimental three-hour capture, because the
remaining inventory, the release history and post-release cooling are
different questions.

\subsection{Independent single-species comparison: Lobresco et al.}
\label{sec:lobresco}
The Liu et al.\ comparisons above are conditioned on deposit-derived
sources. A cleaner test of the transport and wall-exchange model is
offered by the five PbI$_2$ thermosublimatography experiments of
Lobresco et al.\ \cite{Lobresco2026assessment}, which vary the helium
flow (5, 106 and 540 mL/min) and the duration (6, 60 and 300 s) with
1.37--2.23 mg samples in a 4.8 mm steel column. That study also
reports deposition temperatures from two of its own CFD models: a
temperature-based model calibrated against one reference experiment,
and a vapour-pressure-based Hertz--Knudsen--Schrage model with a
near-wall interaction layer and an effective rate multiplier $C_e$. We
ran the five cases without adjusting any parameter to these data and
compare all three models with the measurements by the paper's own
rule: the deposition temperature is the wall temperature at the first
position, from upstream, where the deposit exceeds 1\% of its peak,
linearly interpolated between 1 cm points.

\paragraph{Inputs.} Table 1 of the paper gives the flows, durations
and sample amounts; its flows are standard volumetric rates at
273.15 K and 101,325 Pa. The wall temperature of each flow rate, the
measured deposition profiles, the sample position and both published
model curves were digitised from its Figs.~13--14 (CC BY 4.0). Applying
the 1\% rule to the digitised profiles reproduces the published
experimental deposition temperatures within \LobDigitised{} K. A wall
temperature table distributed with the T-Flows test case of that study
differs from these published curves: its maximum of 1210 K lies at
0.04 m, whereas the figures peak at 1060--1070 K at 0.13--0.15 m, and
between 0.30 and 0.60 m it differs from them by up to 68--92 K. We use
the published curves. Everything else is our model as described in
\cref{sec:model,sec:numerics}, reduced to one species: an expanding
laminar helium carrier computed with the wall temperature, HKS exchange
with $C_e=\alpha=1$ in series with the half-cell diffusion resistance,
the PbI$_2$--He diffusivity correlation of the same paper, and our own
phase-resolved PbI$_2$ vapour-pressure table. The sample is an
evaporating inventory of the Table 1 amount, 1 cm long and 0.48 mm in
radius at the digitised position; this geometry is an assumption, but
the sample empties in less than 0.5 s, so release is not rate-limiting.
The 0.69 m wedge has \LobCellsProd{} cells at the axial and radial
spacing of the production mesh and \LobCellsCoarse{} cells at that of
the 18,432-cell mesh, with limited-linear advection, a zero-total-flux
inlet that lets vapour diffuse upstream, 2 ms time steps (0.5 ms at
540 mL/min) and the sample removed at the end of the experiment.

\paragraph{Results.} \Cref{tab:lobresco,fig:lobresco} summarise the
comparison. Our onset temperatures differ from the measurements by
\LobDiffA, \LobDiffB, \LobDiffC, \LobDiffD{} and \LobDiffE{} K, an RMS
deviation of \LobRMS{} K, against \LobRMSTemp{} K for the calibrated
temperature-based model and \LobRMSVap{} K for the vapour-pressure-based
model of the paper. The two meshes agree within \LobCoarse{} K. The
experimental decrease of the onset temperature with duration at
106 mL/min is reproduced, with a warm offset of 16--26 K, and the
onsets at 5 and 540 mL/min are 11--14 K too cold. Three of the five
deviations exceed the stated $\pm15$ K experimental temperature
uncertainty. The agreement in profile shape is only moderate: the
computed peaks lie \LobShiftMin--\LobShiftMax{} cm upstream of the
measured peaks, and the absolute profile difference is
\LobLOneMin--\LobLOneMax{} of the sample amount.

Three qualifications apply. First, at 5 mL/min vapour diffuses
upstream against the slow carrier and deposits near the cold inlet;
applied from the inlet, the 1\% rule then picks this small deposit, at
\LobFiveInlet{} K. We apply the rule to the main deposit downstream of
the sample. The paper's vapour-pressure model shows a similar upstream
deposit, so its Table 2 value for this case evidently refers to the
main deposit as well.
Second, the experiments end by stopping the flow, withdrawing the
sample and pulling the column out of the furnace; this transient is not
simulated. Counting the vapour still in the tube at the end time where
it is changes only T5\_60 noticeably, to \LobFiveGas{} K, because
\LobFiveGasFraction\% of its inventory is still gaseous (RMS
\LobRMSGas{} K). Third, next to the sample the computed PbI$_2$ mole
fraction reaches 0.33--0.36, far outside the dilute, passive-carrier
assumption of the model; the boat itself is not represented. The
comparison therefore tests the model in a regime it was not built for
near the source, and it rests on digitised rather than tabulated wall
temperatures. It is an independent comparison with published
measurements and published models, not a validation.
