\section{Conditional comparison with deposition measurements}
\label{sec:comparison}
This section compares the calculations, mainly the original Q1 cases,
with the reconstructed deposition scans of Liu et al., and the
single-species model with the experiments of Lobresco et al. The
comparisons are conditional on the reconstructed inputs and assumptions
of \cref{sec:experiments,sec:model}.

\subsection{Comparison measures}
Against experiment we use reconstructed relative iodine
yields per 1 cm bin. Let $e_b$ be the experimental percentage and let
\begin{equation}
 m_b=(100-y_{\rm KI})\frac{a_b}{\sum_{\rm full\ column}a_b}
 \label{eq:yield}
\end{equation}
be the modelled percentage, where the stated excluded KI yield is
$y_{\rm KI}=0$, 6.6 or 7.0. Over the common scanned bins $\mathcal B$,
we report the raw discrepancy
\begin{equation}
 L_{\rm raw}=\sum_{b\in\mathcal B}|m_b-e_b|
 \quad\text{(percentage points)},
 \label{eq:raw-l1}
\end{equation}
which keeps the experimental digitisation sum and the model's explicit
KI exclusion. A secondary shape diagnostic normalises both profiles over
$\mathcal B$, $\widehat m_b=m_b/\sum_{\mathcal B}m_b$ and
$\widehat e_b=e_b/\sum_{\mathcal B}e_b$, and computes the overlap
\begin{equation}
 O=100\sum_{b\in\mathcal B}\min(\widehat m_b,\widehat e_b)
 =100\Bigl[1-\tfrac12\sum_{b\in\mathcal B}|\widehat m_b-\widehat e_b|\Bigr].
 \label{eq:overlap}
\end{equation}
Identical binned shapes give 100\% and disjoint shapes zero. $O$
discards differences in capture and total yield, and its experimental
normalisation still contains KI. $L_{\rm raw}$, $O$ and the
simulation-to-simulation change $E_{L1}$ (\cref{eq:numeric-l1}) have
different denominators and are not interchangeable percentages of model
accuracy.

\subsection{Q1 peaks and complete profiles}
\Cref{tab:peaks} compares the five available Q1 iodide maxima with the
printed peak temperatures. Besides the 1 cm binned maximum it lists the
maximum of the 3 cm smoothed profile ($x_3$, $T_3$) and the wall
temperature at the deposit centroid ($T_{\rm c}$). All five binned peak
temperatures lie within the printed ranges, but the other two measures
do not always. For pure PbI$_2$ the binned maximum (36 cm,
366.5\,$^\circ$C) matches the published 36 cm and 368\,$^\circ$C; the
smoothed maximum (350.5\,$^\circ$C) lies at the lower limit of the
printed $368\pm18$\,$^\circ$C, and the centroid temperature
(348.7\,$^\circ$C) lies 1.3 K below that 350\,$^\circ$C limit. For steel
PbI$_2$ the binned value is 288.1\,$^\circ$C, the centroid temperature
(274.5\,$^\circ$C) lies near the lower limit of $288\pm18$\,$^\circ$C,
and the smoothed maximum (269.2\,$^\circ$C) lies 0.8 K below its
270\,$^\circ$C limit. The steel BiI$_3$ smoothed maximum
(138.8\,$^\circ$C) falls just below its range, while the centroid
temperature (158.1\,$^\circ$C) lies inside it. Whether a computed peak
temperature agrees with a printed range therefore depends on which
measure of the peak is used (\cref{sec:verification}). Steel LBE places
both iodide maxima at the measured positions, whereas silica LBE places
the PbI$_2$ and BiI$_3$ maxima 5 and 4 cm downstream; agreement with a
printed temperature does not establish the spatial location.

The Q1 steel BiI$_3$ share of deposited iodide iodine is 65.34\%, close
to the measured iodide-only share of $61.3/(61.3+32.1)=65.63\%$; for
silica the values are 28.68\% and $27/(27+66)=29.03\%$. Because the
Q1 source masses were inferred from the same deposits, these agreements
cannot serve as independent tests of gas speciation or LBE release.
Similarly, the source-driven calculations capture 91.28--98.20\% of the
supplied iodine on the wall by 60 s, which says nothing about the
experimental three-hour capture.

\subsection{Single-species comparison: Lobresco et al.}
\label{sec:lobresco}
The five PbI$_2$ thermosublimatography experiments of Lobresco et al.\
\cite{Lobresco2026assessment} vary the helium flow (5, 106 and
540 mL/min) and the duration (6, 60 and 300 s) with 1.37--2.23 mg
samples in a 4.8 mm steel column. Unlike the Liu et al.\ cases, they do
not require a deposit-derived source. That study also reports the
deposition temperatures of two of its own CFD models: a
temperature-based model calibrated against one reference experiment,
and a vapour-pressure-based Hertz--Knudsen--Schrage model with a
near-wall interaction layer and an effective rate multiplier $C_e$. We
ran the five cases without adjusting any parameter to these data.

\paragraph{Comparison region.} The deposition temperature is defined as
in that study: the wall temperature at the first position where the
deposit exceeds 1\% of its peak, linearly interpolated between 1 cm
points. We apply this rule to the measured profiles from their first
digitised point, and to our computed deposits downstream of the end of
the sample, in all five cases. We take the rule to locate the onset of
the main deposit formed by transport down the gradient, and the
measured profiles contain only that deposit: the measured T5\_60 profile, digitised from 2 cm, has no
deposit above the threshold upstream of 37 cm. For four cases the
restriction makes no difference. At 5 mL/min, however, computed vapour
diffuses upstream against the slow carrier through the zero-total-flux
inlet condition (below), and \LobFiveUpstream\% of the T5\_60 deposit
forms upstream of the sample, near the cold inlet. Applied from the
inlet, the rule picks this deposit at \LobFiveInlet{} K instead of the
main-deposit onset at \LobFiveMain{} K, and the RMS deviation of our
five onsets becomes \LobRMSInlet{} K instead of \LobRMS{} K. The T5\_60
value, and with it the RMS, therefore depends on the choice of
comparison region; the computed upstream deposit is itself a
discrepancy with the measurement.

\paragraph{Inputs.} Flows (standard volumetric rates at 273.15 K and
101,325 Pa), durations and sample amounts are those of Table 1 of that
study. The wall temperature of each flow rate, the measured profiles,
the sample position and both published model curves were digitised from
its Figs.~13--14 (CC BY 4.0); the 1\% rule applied to the digitised
measured profiles reproduces the published experimental deposition
temperatures within \LobDigitised{} K. The model is that of
\cref{sec:model,sec:numerics} reduced to one species: an expanding
laminar helium carrier computed with the wall temperature, HKS exchange
with $C_e=\alpha=1$ in series with the half-cell diffusion resistance,
the PbI$_2$--He diffusivity correlation of that study, our own
phase-resolved PbI$_2$ vapour-pressure table, an inlet condition of zero
total species flux, and an evaporating sample of the Table 1 amount at
the digitised position, removed at the end of the experiment. Mesh
spacings follow the production and 18,432-cell meshes
(\LobCellsProd{} and \LobCellsCoarse{} cells), with limited-linear
advection; details, including a wall-temperature table of the T-Flows
test case that differs from the published curves, are in Sec.~S6 of the
supplementary material. Our calculation thus differs from both
published models in more than the wall treatment: they prescribe a
fully developed laminar velocity profile, take the PbI$_2$ vapour
pressure from a gas--solid equilibrium calculation, and represent the
source as a timed inlet release (temperature-based model) or a
volumetric solid inventory (vapour-pressure model).

\paragraph{Results.} \Cref{tab:lobresco,fig:lobresco} summarise the
comparison. Our onset temperatures differ from the measurements by
\LobDiffA, \LobDiffB, \LobDiffC, \LobDiffD{} and \LobDiffE{} K, an RMS
deviation of \LobRMS{} K, against \LobRMSTemp{} K for the calibrated
temperature-based model and \LobRMSVap{} K for the vapour-pressure-based
model. The two meshes agree within \LobCoarse{} K. The measured decrease
of the onset temperature with duration at 106 mL/min is reproduced with
a warm offset of 16--26 K; the onsets at 5 and 540 mL/min are 11--14 K
too cold. Three of the five deviations exceed the stated $\pm15$ K
experimental temperature uncertainty. The profiles agree less well than
the onsets: the computed peaks lie \LobShiftMin--\LobShiftMax{} cm
upstream of the measured peaks, and the summed absolute profile
difference is \LobLOneMin--\LobLOneMax{} of the sample amount
(\cref{tab:lobresco}).

\paragraph{Limits of the comparison.} Three limits restrict what the
onset agreement can show. First, the comparison-region choice above
decides the T5\_60 result. Second, the near-source state lies outside
the model: next to the sample the computed PbI$_2$ mole fraction
reaches 0.33--0.36, which violates the dilute, frozen-carrier
assumption (the vapour would alter density, velocity and diffusion),
and the boat is not represented. Third, the release history is not
constrained. In the model the sample is fully evaporated before the end
of even the shortest, 6 s experiment: by then \LobSixDeposit\% of the T106\_6
sample is deposited and the rest is gaseous. Lobresco et al.\ report
that complete evaporation was confirmed in the experiments and that the
evidence indicates near-complete evaporation within the shortest
experiment, which supports release within 6 s. No measurement resolves
the release on shorter time scales; the heat-up of the inserted boat
and the actual sample surface are unknown, and the computed release
rate comes from the non-dilute region where the model does not apply.
The experiments also end by stopping the flow, withdrawing the sample
and pulling the column out of the furnace, which is not simulated;
counting the vapour still in the tube at the end time changes only
T5\_60 noticeably, to \LobFiveGas{} K, because \LobFiveGasFraction\% of
its inventory is still gaseous (RMS \LobRMSGas{} K).

The onset comparison is therefore promising but conditional. Carrier,
vapour-pressure and source assumptions differ from those of the
published models, so the smaller RMS deviation cannot yet be attributed
to the wall treatment, and the profile discrepancies above remain. It
is an untuned comparison with published measurements and published
model results, not a validation. In the channel sweep of
\cref{sec:regime} the 1\% onset does not move when the accommodation
coefficient changes by six decades, so this metric reflects mainly where
the gas saturates, which depends on the vapour pressure, carrier and
source, rather than the interfacial kinetics.
