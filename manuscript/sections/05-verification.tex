\section{Numerical verification and resolution limits}
\label{sec:verification}
This section verifies the wall-exchange discretisation on a problem with
a reference solution, then reports material conservation and the
time-step, iteration, mesh and scheme dependence of the iodine
calculations.

\subsection{Verification of the wall-exchange discretisation}
\label{sec:graetz}
The half-cell series law of \cref{eq:halfcell} is verified on an
extended Graetz problem with a Robin wall condition
\cite{Shah1978circular}: fully developed laminar flow in a pipe,
axial diffusion included, and a constant first-order wall uptake
$-\rho D\,\partial Y/\partial r=\rho k_wY$ at $r=R$. Far from the inlet
and the outlet every cross-sectional measure decays as
$\exp(-\mu x)$, and the decay rate depends only on the P\'eclet number
$\mathrm{Pe}=U_bR/D$ and the Biot number $\mathrm{Bi}=k_wR/D$, the
analogue of the wall Damk\"ohler number of \cref{eq:damkohler}. The
reference $\mu R$ for $\mathrm{Pe}=5$ and $\mathrm{Bi}=1$ and 50 is the
smallest positive eigenvalue of the radial problem, computed by a power
series in extended precision, by shooting and by Chebyshev
collocation; the three agree within a relative $\GraetzBound$.

The test uses the solver's own wall-exchange implementation with a
constant uptake velocity $k_w$ ($R=2.4$ mm, $T=500$ K, $p=1$ atm,
$D=7.6963\times10^{-5}\units{m^2\,s^{-1}}$, a pipe of length $24R$). The
meshes are 2$^\circ$ wedges with exactly axisymmetric cell metrics and
$N_r=32$, 64, 128 and 256 uniform radial cells, $\Delta x=2\Delta r$, and
the parabolic carrier enters through exact face fluxes. Steady states
are obtained by implicit Euler steps with a very large time step, and
$\mu$ is fitted to the computed gas line density in a window chosen from
the spectrum. Two wall laws are compared: the half-cell series law and
the kinetic law applied directly to the cell-centre value (no half-cell
resistance), which is the form of a volumetric first-cell sink. Every
fitted $\mu$ equals the decay rate of the developed discrete mode of
the documented scheme within $\GraetzMuH$, and moving the fit window or
changing the outlet condition changes it by less than $10^{-7}$, so the
differences below are discretisation errors. All \GraetzCriteria{}
acceptance criteria of the study pass (records, provenance and
regeneration: Sec.~S7 of the supplementary material).

\Cref{tab:graetz} gives the relative error of $\mu$ on each mesh, the
observed order and the grid convergence index GCI$_{\rm fine}$ of Celik
et al.\ \cite{Celik2008procedure} on $N_r=64/128/256$. The half-cell law
converges at order \GraetzOrderHalfMin--\GraetzOrderHalfMax{} with
GCI$_{\rm fine}\le\GraetzGCIHalfMax\%$, and its Richardson-extrapolated
decay rate matches the reference within $\GraetzExtHalfMax$. The
cell-centre law converges at first order
(\GraetzOrderNoneMin--\GraetzOrderNoneMax) with
GCI$_{\rm fine}=\GraetzGCINoneMin$--\GraetzGCINoneMax\%. With bounded
SuperBee advection the observed orders are \GraetzOrderSBHalf{} and
\GraetzOrderSBNone{}, respectively (supplementary material, Table~S4).
On the coarsest mesh the cell-centre law errs by 1--2\% in the decay
rate, the half-cell law by at most $10^{-4}$.

This test verifies the discretisation of the wall exchange and its
coupling to advection and diffusion for a linear wall law at
$\mathrm{Bi}\le50$. It does not verify the nonlinear HKS law with
tabulated equilibrium pressures, the gas equilibrium or the wall
inventories, and it does not show that the full iodine calculations are
mesh-converged; their resolution is assessed separately below. The
iodide Damk\"ohler numbers of \cref{sec:regime} also exceed the tested
Biot numbers, and the meshes of the iodine calculations have cell
aspect ratios far from the $\Delta x=2\Delta r$ of the test.

\subsection{Material balance and solver diagnostics}
\label{sec:conservation}
All 15 runnable baseline cases, all 64 follow-up cases, the 19
production cases and the three full-run corrector cases completed and
passed their configured numerical acceptance checks; the scheme and
mesh runs completed with the same ledgers. This PASS classification is
not an experimental-validation classification. For element $k$ the raw
material residue is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 R_k={}&N_k^{\rm gas}+N_k^{\rm wall}+N_k^{\rm sample}
     +N_k^{\rm net\,out}+N_k^{\rm removed}\\
     &-N_k^{\rm initial}-N_k^{\rm released},
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:balance}
\end{equation}
where $N_k$ are molar atom inventories and outward transport is signed;
the linear-solve defect and clamping corrections are recorded separately
and are not inserted into $R_k$. Divided by the supplied material and
maximised over steps and elements, the largest raw residue is
$\ProductionResidue$ for the 19 production cases and
$8.85\times10^{-13}$ for the 60 source-driven follow-up cases. The
calculations conserve the represented elements to round-off; this says
nothing about physical accuracy or discretisation error. The ledger
terms, the equilibrium-solver diagnostics, the startup sign flags and
the trace-loading bound are given in Sec.~S2 of the supplementary
material.

Some paired wall-coupling iterations do not meet the requested
$10^{-16}$ outer criterion. Three cases were therefore repeated over the
full 60 s with eight instead of three correctors (18,432 cells, upwind).
The iodine profiles of pure PbI$_2$ and of the steel Q2 case at 0.1\%
saturation change by $\IterPure\%$ and $\IterSteelLow\%$, that is, at
round-off; the steel case at 1\% saturation changes by
\IterSteelHigh\%. The requested tolerance lies below the attainable
residual floor (Sec.~S2), and the iteration error of the reported
profiles is negligible next to the time-step and spatial errors below.

\subsection{Time-step, mesh and scheme dependence}
For profiles on the common 1 cm bins, let $a_b$ be the deposited
iodine atoms in bin $b$ for a comparison case and $a_b^0$ the
corresponding reference inventory. We report the profile change
$E_{L1}$ and the total change $\Delta N_I$:
\begin{equation}
 E_{L1}=100\frac{\sum_b|a_b-a_b^0|}{\sum_b a_b^0},
 \qquad
 \Delta N_I=100\frac{\sum_b a_b-\sum_b a_b^0}{\sum_b a_b^0}.
 \label{eq:numeric-l1}
\end{equation}
Both measures retain changes in amount. Source, physical coefficients,
equilibrium engine and executable are fixed within each numerical
comparison.

\Cref{tab:numerical} separates time-step from mesh and scheme effects;
for successive mesh levels the reference $a_b^0$ is the finer level.
Reducing the time step from 2 to 1 ms on 18,432 cells changes the
iodine profile by \TimeMin--0.3228\%. With the upwind scheme of the
original campaign, the 18,432 to 41,472-cell differences of the four
follow-up cases are \MeshMin--\MeshMax\%, while total deposited iodine
changes by less than 0.014\% and the centroid moves by at most
0.0652 cm. Integral quantities are thus stable while local profile
shapes still differ appreciably.

The scheme and mesh study (\cref{tab:numerical,fig:refinement})
addresses the shifted-BiI steel case, which had the largest upwind
change. With limited-linear advection its iodine profile changes by
\SteelLLOne, \SteelLLTwo{} and \SteelLLThree\% over the three
refinements to 93,312 cells; with upwind, by \SteelUpOne, \SteelUpTwo{}
and \SteelUpThree\%. Pure PbI$_2$ changes by \PureLLOne{} and
\PureLLTwo\% with limited-linear, against \PureUpOne{} and \PureUpTwo\%
with upwind. The van Leer scheme differs from limited-linear by at most
\VLSteelMax\% (steel) and \VLPureMax\% (pure PbI$_2$) at equal mesh.
Between 41,472 and 93,312 cells, the 3 cm smoothed profile of the steel
case changes by \SteelSmoothThree\% and its total deposit by
\SteelTotalThree\%; over all four levels its centroid stays within
\SteelCentroidRange{} mm and the wall temperature at the PbI$_2$
centroid within \SteelCentroidTRange{} K.

The successive limited-linear profile changes of the steel case give
apparent orders of \SteelOrderOne{} and \SteelOrderTwo, and its iodine
total, centroid and width oscillate on both triplets. These orders are
inconsistent, so no formal grid convergence index is claimed
\cite{NASAGridConvergence,Roache1994perspective,Celik2008procedure}. We
quote the measured 41,472 to 93,312-cell change of \SteelLLThree\% as the
spatial uncertainty indicator of the production profiles. Extrapolating
the last change at the last apparent order would place the 41,472-cell
profile about \SteelErrApparent\% from the mesh limit, and assuming
second order about \SteelErrSecond\%. These are indicative estimates for
one case, not confirmed bounds, and they do not establish mesh
independence of local profile shapes in other cases. The three-grid
scalar checks of the earlier upwind triplets are in Sec.~S3 of the
supplementary material.

At equal mesh, upwind differs from limited-linear by \UpLLOne, \UpLLTwo,
\UpLLThree{} and \UpLLFour\% for the steel case on the four levels and
by \PureUpLLMin--\PureUpLLMax\% for pure PbI$_2$, much more than the two
bounded schemes differ from each other. This is consistent with the
local estimate $D_{\rm num}\simeq|u_x|\Delta x/2$, which is comparable
to or greater than the prescribed $10^{-4}\units{m^2\,s^{-1}}$ in parts
of the carriers. Upwind numerical diffusion is therefore the dominant
spatial error of the original campaign, and it is why halving the time
step could not establish convergence of profile widths. Across the 19
production cases, the production profiles differ from the 18,432-cell
upwind profiles by 9.1--22.3\%. The follow-up sensitivities of
\cref{sec:sensitivity} remain upwind differences on 18,432 cells, taken
against baselines with the same numerical error.

\subsection{Binned maxima and observation width}
A deposition maximum picked on a 1 cm grid is fragile when adjacent
bins have similar heights. In pure PbI$_2$ the 18,432-cell upwind
maximum is at 37 cm and 350.7\,$^\circ$C, while the 36 cm bin is only
about 0.05\% lower; on 41,472 cells it moves to 36 cm and
366.5\,$^\circ$C although the centroid shifts by only $-0.0585$ cm. In
the limited-linear steel sequence the PbI$_2$ maximum alternates between
two nearly equal bins, 50 and 51 cm (306.3, 288.1, 306.7 and
288.1\,$^\circ$C on the four levels), while the 3 cm smoothed maximum
stays at 51 cm and 288.0--288.1\,$^\circ$C and the centroid temperature
at 290.6--291.5\,$^\circ$C. A one-bin switch can change the reported
peak temperature by tens of kelvin without a comparable displacement of
the profile, so \cref{tab:peaks} also reports the smoothed and centroid
values. Conversely, comparing profiles on wider bins does not remove the
mesh sensitivity: for the shifted-BiI case the original upwind
fine/medium difference on 4 cm bins ranges from 0.52\% to 10.02\%
depending on the bin origin (Sec.~S4 of the supplementary material,
which also gives a transport distance between the binned profiles).
