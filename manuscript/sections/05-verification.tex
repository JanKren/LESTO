\section{Numerical verification and resolution limits}
\label{sec:verification}
\subsection{Completion, material balance and solver diagnostics}
\label{sec:conservation}
All 15 runnable baseline cases and all 64 follow-up cases completed
with their configured numerical acceptance checks. A PASS classification
means that the computation completed and passed those checks; it is not
an experimental-validation classification. Element conservation is
examined both through the solver ledger and through an independently
formed material residue. For element $k$ the latter is
\begin{equation}
 R_k=N_k^{\rm gas}+N_k^{\rm wall}+N_k^{\rm sample}
     +N_k^{\rm net\,out}+N_k^{\rm removed}
     -N_k^{\rm initial}-N_k^{\rm released},
 \label{eq:balance}
\end{equation}
where $N_k$ are molar atom inventories and outward transport is signed.
The ledger separately records the linear-solve defect and any clamping
correction. These terms explain bookkeeping closure but are not inserted
into $R_k$ when assessing the raw material balance.

Across recorded steps and represented elements, the baseline's largest
raw residue divided by supplied material is $5.97\times10^{-13}$.
For the 60 source-driven follow-up cases it is
$8.85\times10^{-13}$; the largest accounted closure is
$9.60\times10^{-16}$ and the largest recorded solver defect is
$8.64\times10^{-13}$ on the same supplied-material scale.
The four flow-stop cases retain their restart ledger and are excluded
from this newly accumulated-source summary. These results support
conservation of the executed discrete model. They do not quantify
physical accuracy or discretisation error.

The baseline logs report no gas-equilibrium solver failures and a
maximum equilibrium element residue of $6.43\times10^{-16}$.
Input-validity and solver-success counters are distinct: startup
sign flags caused equilibrium to be skipped in up to 1,170 cells on
one step. The maximum negative iodine diagnostic was
$1.14\times10^{-124}$ of supplied iodine. Matched five-step startup
replays identified extremely small leading-tail inventories at these
flags, with no flagged cells remaining at the final step. Thus the
calculations should not be described as having identically zero negative
trace values, even though the reported amount is immaterial to the
macroscopic balance. The sum of individual species mole-fraction maxima
was at most $9.19\times10^{-5}$; since these maxima need not coincide,
this is an upper bound on the reactive trace loading, not a measured
mixture mole fraction at one location.

Some paired wall-coupling iterations did not meet the requested
$10^{-16}$ outer criterion. This is reported separately from equilibrium
failure, the final transport defect and inventory-bound checks. Matched
restarts of four representative cases at 59 s compared three and eight
correctors and outer tolerances of $10^{-16}$ and $10^{-14}$ for
20 time steps, or 40 ms. The largest species-field change, scaled by
its control maximum, was below $3.6\times10^{-8}$, with negligible new
wall-increment changes. This supports a residual-floor interpretation
at the tested checkpoints, but leaves full-run iteration sensitivity
unqualified. Planned manufactured-solution and cross-code comparisons
are not presented as completed verification evidence.

\subsection{Separate time-step and mesh comparisons}
For profiles on the common 1 cm bins, let $a_b$ be deposited iodine
atoms in bin $b$ for a comparison case and $a_b^0$ the corresponding
baseline inventory. We report
\begin{equation}
 E_{L1}=100\frac{\sum_b|a_b-a_b^0|}{\sum_b a_b^0},
 \qquad
 \Delta N_I=100\frac{\sum_b a_b-\sum_b a_b^0}{\sum_b a_b^0}.
 \label{eq:numeric-l1}
\end{equation}
These measures retain amount changes. Normalised shape curves are
shown separately and must not be confused with this unnormalised
profile difference. Source, physical coefficients, equilibrium engine
and executable are fixed within each numerical comparison.

\Cref{tab:numerical} shows that reducing the time step from 2 to 1 ms
on 18,432 cells changes the iodine profile by 0.0043--0.3228\%.
The fine/medium mesh differences at a fixed 2 ms are 2.06--10.58\%.
The shifted-BiI steel case has essentially the same magnitude of
profile change on successive refinements. Its spatial error is therefore
not resolved by the time-step result. Total deposited iodine changes
by less than 0.014\% from medium to fine, and the centroid changes by
at most 0.0652 cm. Stable integrals coexist with appreciable differences
in local profile shape.

Three-grid scalar checks follow the distinction between refinement
trends and an established asymptotic range \cite{NASAGridConvergence}.
For the 34 nontrivial or zero-valued combinations of species, total,
centroid and width, 17 sequences are oscillatory, four have nondecreasing
differences, one is unresolved at roundoff and 12 admit only conditional
Richardson/GCI estimates. All four iodine-total sequences are oscillatory.
The conditional estimates are retained in the accompanying data with
their status; they are not promoted to confirmed uncertainty bounds.
The current evidence does not establish mesh independence or a single
observed convergence order for this coupled model.

\subsection{Binned maxima and numerical diffusion}
A deposition maximum selected on a 1 cm grid is particularly fragile
when adjacent bins have similar heights. In pure PbI$_2$ the medium-grid
maximum is at 37 cm and 350.7\,$^\circ$C, while the 36 cm bin is only
about 0.05\% lower. The fine-grid maximum moves to 36 cm and
366.5\,$^\circ$C despite a centroid shift of only $-0.0585$ cm.
In the steel Q2 0.1\% case, the dominant BiI$_3$ bin changes from
57 to 58 cm, moving its reported temperature from about 166.4 to
138.8\,$^\circ$C. A one-bin switch can therefore create a temperature
change of nearly 28 K without a comparable displacement of the profile
as a whole. Peak temperature alone is an insufficient convergence metric.

First-order upwind advection also adds spatial smoothing. The local
one-dimensional estimate $D_{\rm num}\simeq|u_x|\Delta x/2$ is
comparable to or greater than the prescribed $10^{-4}\units{m^2\,s^{-1}}$
diffusivity in parts of the baseline carriers. This is a Taylor-series
screening estimate, not a measured truncation error for the radial,
variable-density coupled problem. It explains why halving the time
step cannot establish convergence of profile widths. Further spatial
refinement or a separately verified bounded higher-order transport
scheme is needed before precise profile-shape prediction is claimed.
