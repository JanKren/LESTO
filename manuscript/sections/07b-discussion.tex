\section{Discussion: what the evidence supports}
\label{sec:discussion}
\subsection{Conservation, resolution and experimental agreement}
The results separate three requirements that a deposition calculation
must satisfy. Element balance tests the consistency of the discrete
transport--equilibrium--wall coupling; the Graetz--Robin test verifies
the wall discretisation against a reference solution; resolution
studies test how much the computed observables depend on numerical
choices; and experimental comparison tests the combined consequences of
the numerical model, its physical assumptions and its input data.
Passing the first two does not establish the others.

Here the raw material residues are at round-off and the half-cell wall
law converges at second order, yet local profile shapes remain
resolution-sensitive while integrals are stable, and nearly equal bins
can shift a reported peak temperature by tens of kelvin. Profile
differences, centroids and deposited amounts should therefore accompany
peak comparisons. The physical-input responses are finite responses over
selected cases; they cannot be added in quadrature or read as a
universal parameter ranking, and several are comparable in magnitude to
the unresolved spatial differences.

\subsection{What the experimental comparisons can identify}
Q1 uses deposit-derived compound masses for LBE, so agreement in the
iodide split partly reflects information supplied to the calculation.
Q2 lets the split emerge but combines a deposit-derived iodine loading
with an unmeasured metal saturation; the computed split responds
strongly to both the BiI thermodynamic input and the source loading, so
one observed split constrains several coupled assumptions rather than
identifying one of them. The silica scan and its printed temperatures are
mutually inconsistent, and the two diagnostic shifts expose rather than
resolve this. The untuned Lobresco comparison gives onset temperatures
closer to the measurements than both published models, but with
different carrier, vapour-pressure and source assumptions, a
rule-dependent T5\_60 value and profiles displaced upstream; it is
encouraging for the transport and wall law, not a demonstration of
them. The practical contribution is therefore a framework for testing
explicit input hypotheses while retaining conservation and exposing
profile discrepancies. Fitting an interfacial coefficient to a single
peak under these conditions could compensate for an error elsewhere in
the model.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
The limitations are collected here and referred to elsewhere.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Physics not represented:} a frozen, trace-loaded carrier
  with the wall temperature imposed over each cross-section, so no
  buoyancy-driven secondary flow (possibly significant locally in a
  horizontal column; orientation unknown), no conjugate heat transfer
  and no non-dilute effects near a source; no aerosols, nucleation,
  Soret transport, KI or water/oxygen chemistry, substrate reactions or
  boat obstruction; instantaneous ideal-gas equilibrium and an
  irreversible BiI wall channel in place of unmeasured kinetics.
\item \emph{Inputs:} digitised rather than tabulated wall temperatures;
  an unconfirmed coordinate origin and the silica inconsistency;
  assumed controller reference conditions and column pressure;
  deposit-conditioned source masses and an unmeasured metal saturation;
  BiI and constructed BiI$_3$(l) thermodynamic records that need
  independent qualification.
\item \emph{Protocol:} 60 s source-driven runs at the three-hour mean
  release rate, flow-stop runs at fixed temperature, and no simulation
  of the cooling or withdrawal at the end of any experiment.
\item \emph{Numerics:} spatial qualification beyond one refined case,
  a consistent observed order for the iodine profiles, production-scheme
  repeats of the follow-up categories other than the diffusivity and
  accommodation variations of two baselines, a reference-solution
  verification of the HKS law with tabulated equilibrium pressures (the
  channel sweep checks only its limits) are outstanding.
\end{itemize}
Each item addresses an ambiguity demonstrated by the completed cases.
The most direct experimental constraints would be the original
thermocouple and scan coordinates, controller reference conditions, an
independently measured release history and the column orientation and
pressure. None of the omitted effects can be inferred from the present
sensitivity ranges.
